# Corporate indifference

Arasaka is not personal villain. It does not need to hate Mari, Sanya, or Eli to harm them. It pursues institutional continuity, research value, ownership, and power at a scale where individual people become incidental costs—ants beneath a larger agenda.

This makes Arasaka's help conditional, impersonal, and morally insufficient. Individual employees may still act with care; system does not. Keep corporation pressure present without turning story into conspiracy-action thriller.

Late in story, Arasaka becomes a serious apparent final obstacle: Sanya needs its transition infrastructure before her neural-map window closes, while its terms would reduce her continued self to owned research property. Nadia averts that outcome. This is a genuine external victory, but deliberately not a happy resolution: Sanya gains autonomy while Mari still loses her biological presence.
